Start with the basics

Meal prep without the stress

Meal prep does not mean cooking every meal for the entire week in one day. It simply means doing a little work ahead of time so everyday cooking becomes easier. Start with a few small habits and build a routine that fits your schedule.

Four steps to get started

1Choose your meals

Look at your upcoming schedule and choose two or three meals you want to prepare. Pick recipes that share ingredients, such as rice, chicken, beans, or vegetables.

2Make a grocery list

Check your refrigerator, freezer, and pantry first. Write down only what you need, organize the list by food category, and avoid buying ingredients you already have.

3Prepare ingredients

Wash and chop produce, measure seasonings, and cook a batch of rice or another grain. Keep raw meat separate from ready-to-eat foods and clean your work surfaces.

4Store and label

Divide cooked food into clean, shallow containers. Label each container with its contents and preparation date, then refrigerate or freeze it promptly.

Useful meal prep essentials

You do not need an expensive kitchen setup. A few reliable tools are enough to get started.

Food storage containers

Choose food-safe, leak-resistant containers with secure lids. Different sizes help you store individual portions, cooked grains, chopped vegetables, and sauces separately.

A sharp kitchen knife

A suitable kitchen knife and a stable cutting board make chopping produce easier. Store knives safely and wash cutting boards after preparing raw meat.

Measuring tools

Measuring cups, spoons, and a kitchen scale can help you portion ingredients and follow recipes consistently. A food thermometer helps you check that food reaches a safe temperature.

Food storage and safety

Keep your prepared food safe

  • Refrigerate perishable food within two hours of cooking, or within one hour when temperatures are above 90°F.
  • Keep your refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below and your freezer at 0°F (-18°C) or below.
  • Use most cooked leftovers within three to four days when properly refrigerated, or freeze portions for later.
  • Reheat leftovers to 165°F (74°C), checking the center with a food thermometer.
  • Cook chicken to an internal temperature of 165°F (74°C).
  • Never leave cooked rice or other perishable food sitting at room temperature for extended periods.
  • Thaw frozen food in the refrigerator, in cold water using safe procedures, or in the microwave rather than on the counter.

When in doubt about how long food has been stored or whether it was kept at a safe temperature, discard it rather than tasting it to check.

Your first 30-minute prep session

You can get a useful head start without spending the whole afternoon in the kitchen. Try this simple routine:

  • Minutes 0–5: Review your meals and gather ingredients and containers.
  • Minutes 5–15: Wash produce, chop vegetables, and measure seasonings.
  • Minutes 15–25: Start cooking a grain or preparing ingredients for your first dinner.
  • Minutes 25–30: Label containers, clean your workspace, and put ingredients away safely.

Use this as a starting routine and adjust it to match your recipes and cooking times.

Keep building your routine

Once you are comfortable preparing a few ingredients ahead of time, try planning a full week of dinners. Our Meal Plans page gives you a five-day starting point, while Meal Ideas offers simple recipes for breakfast, lunch, and dinner.
